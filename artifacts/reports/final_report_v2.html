CONCORDIA — Final Validation, Alignment Frontier, and Conditional RL

Claim boundary. Every traffic experiment is synthetic. The real-network study uses real OSM geometry with synthetic OD demand. TTC/DRAC/PET are surrogate conflict indicators, never crash probabilities. Route changes occur only after modeled acceptance.

1. Initial hypotheses

CONCORDIA tested whether preference heterogeneity and voluntary, acceptance-aware route recommendations could improve traffic efficiency without worsening surrogate safety or forcing individual sacrifice.

3. Efficiency–voluntariness trade-off

Study I evaluated 360 seed/topology/demand/heterogeneity frontiers and 3240 epsilon points. All frontiers were monotone; the knee epsilon median was 0.020. Sampled regions: {'INFEASIBLE': 580, 'TRADEOFF': 1335, 'WIN': 1325}.

H1-R did not show that heterogeneity is uniformly a coordination resource: weighted alignment opportunity was 20009.2 in the low group versus 9960.5 in the high group. Preference diversity helps only when available route attributes expose useful time, reliability, or risk trade-offs; topology can remove that choice space.

4. Price of Alignment

PoAlign(epsilon) = C*(epsilon) / C_SO quantifies the system cost of preserving the declared regret budget. Marginal finite differences report how much TTT is recovered per additional unit of permitted private loss.

The acceptance–traffic fixed point converged in 15/ 15 cases. FP1 reduced mean acceptance Brier score from 0.02888 to 0.02839, at the cost of a slower analytical solve.

6. Real-topology transfer

20 SUMO runs used 3 legal alternatives on the committed Gangnam OSM extract. Transfer and limited-retuning results are separate. Demand provenance remains synthetic demand on real topology.

Transfer failed to improve TTT: B1=8097.2s and B6=8403.0s. The failure decomposition records mean route-overlap Jaccard 0.680, mean accepted-route preference cost 0.0089, and 0 loaded secondary bottleneck edges. This is mechanism failure on synthetic demand, not an observed Seoul effect.

7. Scalability and RL Gate

B6's first declared enumeration-limit failure was {'candidate_count': 8, 'user_count': 6}. The pre-RL mathematical approximation reached operational p95 0.1139s; residual Gate E trigger: False. Gate C median incremental degradation was 0.0694 against a 0.10 threshold. Its p95 was 1.0821, retained as a tail failure even though the pre-registered median Gate C did not authorize RL.

Gate B nevertheless authorized RL0. Its held-out mean TTT gap versus the constrained deterministic comparator was 0.00000, p95 inference was 0.000034s, and regret/safety violations were 0/0. It was rejected because it did not outperform the deterministic comparator.

8. Conditions of validity and failure

  • Alignment Win: regret-feasible recommendations beat sampled ETA-only cost.
  • Alignment Trade-off: voluntariness is protected but network cost is higher.
  • Alignment Infeasible: the permitted utility loss contains no useful diversion.
  • Microscopic failure: B6 increased VALID event incidence in this matrix and did not establish DRAC-CVaR non-inferiority.
  • Topology-transfer failure: accepted recommendations redistributed demand but increased TTT on the tested real geometry.
